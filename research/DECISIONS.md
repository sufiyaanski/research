# Decisions — motion toolkit (branch `motion-toolkit-2026-11-02`)

Each entry: what was decided, and why. Newest at the bottom.

1. **Decided: branch `motion-toolkit-2026-11-02` from `claude/tender-turing-2464tr`, not from `main`** (because that branch already holds the research files 00–05, the Remotion skills and the 3D blocky-character rig the toolkit reuses; `main` has only a README). Nothing is merged to main.
2. **Decided: the new toolkit lives in `toolkit/` as its own npm project, separate from `setup/`** (because the new spec differs from the older kit: 30 fps instead of 60, cream ground #F4EFE6 instead of the dark Signal palette, a blocky person in every Signal beat. Keeping them apart avoids breaking the older kit's rendered demos). `toolkit/HARNESS.md` is the authority for new reels; where it conflicts with `setup/CLAUDE.md`, HARNESS.md wins.
3. **Decided: YouTube access only via the routes the brief lists, used plainly** (because on 2026-11-02 the cloud IP got "Sign in to confirm you're not a bot" from yt-dlp and `IpBlocked` from youtube-transcript-api, and an attempt to try alternative player clients was refused as bot-check circumvention). So: no client rotation, no cookies, no third-party transcript proxies. Fallbacks in order: plain yt-dlp → youtube-transcript-api → public page/oEmbed/description → web write-ups. Every claim not confirmed from a transcript is marked [U] in `research/videos/`.
4. **Decided: captions block centred at y ≈ 1180 (inside y 1080–1280)** (because "lower-middle third" must also clear the platform UI: Meta's published bottom 35% (y > 1248) and the right action rail; 1180 is the lowest centre that keeps a 2-line 64–72 px block above that zone).
5. **Decided: entrances 8–12 frames, exits 40–60% of the entrance, at 30 fps** (because HyperFrames/LottieFiles measured 0.3–0.6 s entrances for premium motion, and the brief bans wobble; 8–12 f at 30 fps = 0.27–0.4 s keeps the faster short-form pace the reference creators use).
6. **Decided: the same original 3D blocky character (from `setup/remotion/src/lib/character/`) for both channels, re-coloured per channel** (because it already renders deterministically at 1080×1920 in Remotion, is original (no Minecraft/Roblox assets), and the brief asks for the same character on both channels). 3D needs `--gl=swangle` on GPU-less machines.
7. **Decided: code-synthesised SFX and music bed only (Node, no deps), committed as WAVs** (because the brief bans paid APIs, and synthesised sounds are ours with no licence questions). Paid/hosted tools seen in the tutorials (Claude Motion, Pixels MCP, Higgsfield, Fish Audio) are documented, not used.
8. **Decided: commit the example MP4s under `toolkit/out/`** (because the brief asks for rendered examples, and 12–15 s at 30 fps is a few MB each).
9. **Decided: keep the brief's critique (middle frame of every shot; story/composition/motion/type/sync; fix 3 worst; ≥ 8; max 3 rounds) and fold the source method's seven axes into the five definitions** (because the only video that shows the loop (Lukas Margerie, via the Movez course) uses one frame per *beat* and seven axes, and asks for *at least* 3 rounds; the brief's per-shot middle frame maps cleanly onto our shot list, and a max of 3 rounds keeps cost bounded. The Movez hunt list, loop-seam check and determinism hash check were added to HARNESS §4).
10. **Decided: no springs with overshoot, despite the source method using springs (k=170, d=26; tiny UI overshoot)** (because the brief says "no wobble unless asked"; cubic eases are the house default).
11. **Decided: the "crew" line names six roles (director, writer, animator, composer, sound designer, render engineer)** (because the brief asks for it; the source course names only four, with no writer or composer, so this is our extension, not a confirmed creator method).
12. **Decided: don't commit the user-supplied reference reel or frames from it; commit only our measurements and analysis** (because the video belongs to @analyst_dnb; our measured timeline, colours and format notes are enough to recreate the *format* with original content).
13. **Decided: analyse the reel by measurement — OCR of every on-screen text at 0.25 s (RapidOCR), Whisper for speech, ebur128 loudness, a spectrogram and colour sampling** (because "the exact formula" needs exact timings; eyeballing a contact sheet would miss the 11× slowed clock and the subtitle rhythm). Tool attribution ("code-rendered 3D") is an inference and is marked [U].
14. **Decided: the studio operating system lives at the repo root — `START-HERE.md` (human), `CLAUDE.md` (Claude's rules), `studio/` (channels, episodes, STATUS board), `.claude/commands/` (9 slash commands) — and `toolkit/` stays the engine** (because the user wants to say one word (`/go`) and approve at gates; Claude Code loads the root `CLAUDE.md` and `.claude/commands/` automatically when the repo is opened, while the toolkit keeps its own HARNESS/README for render rules). Four human gates (story+facts, shot list, phone check, posting); posting is never automated without an explicit, named, per-pack request (research/03 §1.1 "hard human gates").
15. **Decided: the third short-form channel is a template slot `channel-3` launched by a guided `/new-channel` interview, not a channel picked now** (because the user has many ideas and should choose with the two live channels running; the flow fills a bible, palette, 10 scored ideas and a 6 s style-test render). Ranked candidates that reuse the toolkit: **1. Scale Shock** (everyday things at absurd scale — paper bigNumber/compareBars + the character beside giant 3D objects; cheapest, evergreen, sendable, low fact risk) · **2. Map Moments** (geographic oddities — pins/map diagram + remotion-maps + 3D sets; strong YouTube search value) · **3. Tiny History** (one object's history — timeline/quote templates; highest fact risk and new period sets, so last). Suggested launch: week 49, 2 posts/week.
16. **Decided: the long-form channel is "Q-ROSITY Long": 10–15 min what-if deep dives, published on the Q-ROSITY YouTube channel's long-form tab** (because it reuses everything already built — the blocky character, the 3D night sets, the what-if title/clock grammar — and each episode yields 2–3 vertical Shorts that link back as the "Related video"; one subscriber base instead of a cold start). Rejected: a Signal long-form (price-psychology documentaries — cheaper 2D renders but 10+ sourced studies per episode, and YouTube's "AI persona giving financial advice" rule sits next to that niche); a compilation/"best of" channel (reused-content and mass-production policy risk); a new faceless history/doc channel (no character or set reuse, new brand from zero). Risk logged: crowded "what if" niche — differentiate with sourced step-by-step consequences and recurring Q, and vary domains/outcomes per episode.
17. **Decided: long-form is data-driven and rendered per chapter** — chapters/*.json (scene = template + VO lines + props cued by spoken words) → free voice → `timeline.json` in frames → `LongformChapter` rendered once per chapter with `--props` (hash-skipped when unchanged) → ffmpeg concat (video stream copy; SFX audio padded per chapter to avoid AAC-priming drift) → voice + looped bed sidechain-ducked + SFX → two-pass loudnorm −14 LUFS / −1.5 dBTP (because a 12-min single render can't be resumed or partially fixed, and a VO re-take should not force a picture re-render). No burned-in word captions in 16:9; `captions.srt` from the same timings is uploaded instead. Template library: whatif, narrator (with word-cued `shots`), title, bigNumber, compareBars, diagram (orbit / pins placeholder / flow), timeline, quote, endscreen + a 1280×720 thumbnail.
18. **Decided: long-form voice = Kokoro-82M (`am_michael`, speed 0.85) via `kokoro-onnx`, word timings from faster-whisper `base.en`, ElevenLabs only if the user adds a key** (because it's free, local and the best-sounding engine that installed: measured RTF 0.29–0.37 synthesis + 0.2–0.4 alignment on 4 CPU cores; Piper `en_US-ryan-high` also works (RTF 0.22) but has flatter prosody and stays as fallback; the torch `kokoro` package fails on Python 3.13 because it pins numpy 1.26.4). Kokoro at speed 1.0 narrates ~3 words/s — too fast — so 0.85 gives 2.72 w/s. One narrator voice for every episode; the description credits a synthetic voice.
19. **Decided: starting cadence for one person on Claude Max = 3 Signal + 3 Q-ROSITY shorts a week (each cross-posted to IG/TikTok/YT with native cuts) + 1 long-form every 2 weeks (+ 2–3 Shorts from it); channel-3 from week 49 at 2/week; scale a channel to 5/week only when its median holds and a short takes < 45 min of the user's attention** (because research 02 §9.2 recommends ~6/week per channel, but every piece needs the user at 3 gates (~45–75 min per short) and Claude Max usage is shared across all channels; measured machine time is not the bottleneck: a 45 s 3D short ≈ 8–10 min to render, a 2D Signal short ≈ 3–5 min, the 69.6 s long-form demo 9.5 min end to end (0.25–0.27 s/frame with swangle, concurrency 4), so a 12-min episode = 21,600 frames ≈ 1.5–1.7 h render + critique + chapter re-renders ≈ 2–2.5 h unattended). Estimated user time ≈ 9–10 h/week. Extra volume goes into Trial Reels (once ≥ 1,000 followers), not more public posts.
20. **Decided: shorts episode code lives in `toolkit/episodes/<id>/` (registered in `toolkit/episodes/index.tsx`), episode data/paperwork in `studio/episodes/<id>/`** (because Remotion/webpack resolve `remotion` from `toolkit/node_modules`, so TSX outside `toolkit/` can't import it, while JSON imports from `studio/` work fine). Studio compositions are mounted through one `{/* STUDIO:BEGIN */}…{/* STUDIO:END */}` block in `src/Root.tsx`; `toolkit/longform/studio.tsx` holds them.
21. **Decided: the long-form demo ships after 3 critique rounds at 79/100 with 8 cells still at 7 (none below)** (because HARNESS §4 caps rounds at 3; the remaining issues — static paper scenes after their reveal, a subtle tide-gauge change, empty lower halves on number scenes — are template-level and logged in `toolkit/longform/demo/review_log.md` for the first real episode). The demo's facts were checked against NOAA/NASA pages: the Sun's tide force is "about half" (NOAA) / "a little less than half" (NASA) of the Moon's, so the planned "46 %" became "≈ 50 %", and "roughly a third" is shown as our own arithmetic.
